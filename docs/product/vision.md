# Product Vision

Afterglow is a premium-feeling 18+ web/PWA social card game for adults. It
should support couples and groups of 2-8 players, with a mobile-first experience
that works well on iPhone, Android, and desktop browsers.

## Product Promise

The game should help adults enter a flirtatious, sensual, and playful atmosphere
without feeling rushed or forced by random commands. The core value is the mix
of choice, atmosphere, progression, consent, and interaction between players.

## Core Principles

- Choice: players hold multiple cards and decide what to play.
- Atmosphere: interludes, conversation, music, dance, massage, and group moments
  matter as much as card resolution.
- Progression: spice increases gradually from warmup toward stronger levels.
- Consent: refusal is a normal part of play, and boundaries are hard limits.
- Interaction: players can react, counter, vote, redirect, rescue, and stay
  engaged outside their own turns.
- Premium tone: sensual and elegant rather than crude or pornographic in
  presentation.
- Game-like experience: the frontend should feel immersive, playful, and
  card-first rather than like a traditional form-based application.

## Frontend Experience Direction

The user experience should emphasize dark backgrounds, premium atmosphere,
elegant styling, game-like interactions, gold accents, and burgundy or purple
accents. It should remain mobile-first while still working well on desktop.

Avoid an enterprise or admin-panel appearance.

## Planned Modes

- Party Warmup: lighter, more humorous, more flirting and questions.
- Night of Tension: standard progressive mode.
- Slow Burn: slower, more atmospheric, with more interludes.
- Fantasy Finale: shorter and stronger, only with consent from all players.
- Custom: host-configured levels, categories, pace, props, and boundaries.

## Safety Principles

Each player should confirm adult status, consent to participate, awareness of
erotic content, and personal boundaries before play. Cards should be filtered by
the most restrictive boundaries of the participants involved in a task.
